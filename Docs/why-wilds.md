# Why Wilds?

Game worlds have a habit of becoming more than we originally planned for.

An NPC walks into an art gallery. A fish encounters a fishing lure. A patron finds himself at a poker table with a cranky miner. None of those ideas is especially difficult to imagine. The difficult part is letting them belong naturally to the same world without scripting every possible interaction or teaching the engine every noun the world may someday contain.

**Wilds cannot be your game world, and it does not want to be.**

You know what the gallery means. You decide why the miner is cranky, what the lure does, what an entity believes, and which consequences matter. Wilds works underneath that meaning. It supplies reusable machinery for state, relationships, work, provenance, propagation, history, and knowledge development.

## Surprise without opacity

If emergent behavior surprises you, the right question should be fun: **“Did I code that?”**

The answer should still be traceable. Wilds treats provenance as usable structure, not merely diagnostic history. Consumer-owned values, Evidence, Facts, Discoveries, vector contributions, clusters, scoped Truths, and Consequences retain the attributable structures needed to understand how an available result was produced.

**Wilds may surprise you. It should not make you guess.**

## Bring the ecosystem, not the fish

A finished game leaves its domain behind. The fish, gallery, miner, lore, and rules belong to that world. The reusable simulation and knowledge machinery should not.

The point of Wilds is to let the underlying architecture travel to the next project without bringing the last project's semantic baggage with it.

## The boundary

- QSL processes legitimately available state.
- Wilds Core processes simulation.
- Wilds.Discoveries develops knowledge, narratives, scoped Truths, and Consequences.
- External agency decides what to do next.

**Your world defines the meaning. Wilds gives that meaning room to participate.**
